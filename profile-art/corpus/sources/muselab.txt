I wanted better feedback on my own poetry.
Most AI writing tools gave me the same thing: vague praise, generic craft advice, and a chat box that treated a poem like a support ticket. They could count my repetitions. They rarely told me what the draft was about.
MuseLab is my attempt to fix that—for myself and for anyone else who writes in short forms. You submit a draft. The backend reads it through several lenses (structure, emotion, metaphor, development, references). The UI returns a dossier: folio tabs you flip through, not a conversation you scroll forever.
What makes it different: interpretation over generation. The app is not trying to rewrite your poem. It tries to argue about it—grounded in your lines—and leave you with something you can revise from.
Dossier, not chat. A poem isn't a thread. I wanted the feeling of a workshop packet—sheets you move through at your own pace.
Interpretation, not replacement. Agents name mechanisms in your language so you can revise. I explicitly did not want here's my version of your poem.
Hybrid analysis, not LLM-only. Deterministic features, an emotion classifier, and reference retrieval run before the language-model agents. That grounding keeps feedback from floating off into generic advice.
Two agent systems, kept separate. Poetry agents critique your text. Builder agents critique this repo. Mixing those prompts would blur the product.
Humans stay in the loop on builder output. Builder agents write markdown reports. They do not merge code. I review, edit, reject, then implement.
